## Shot A —5 seconds

Actual24.05m travel, matching wheel odometry, steering/acceleration-driven suspension and geometric driver head rotation replace a purely compositing-level demonstration. Moving houses/lamps are fixed world geometry. Hands remain on the actual steering-wheel circle. However, the sedan shape, simplified head/face and flat shading read as a rough mesh reconstruction. The driver insert is functional rather than cinematic; shoulder/torso acting remains restrained and mechanical. The approved V10 ink treatment and atmospheric light are not matched. No HUD is used to imply speed.

## Shot B —7.8 seconds

The actor travels to the entrance with explicitly held stance targets. Actual hardware supports the failed handle pull, key retrieval/alignment/insertion/turn, hinged opening and entry. A closer view makes the key beat easier to inspect. The motion is still overly controlled: limited elbow/shoulder overlap, stiff torso and repetitive contact timing weaken urgency and weight. The cap/back-of-head silhouette and the camera angle obscure some hand detail; exact endpoint contact does not automatically make the grip readable. The model's face and outline remain off the approved illustrated target.

## Shot C —8 seconds

Three real0.2m risers receive distinct foot plants; the pelvis rises0.60m. The actor approaches, grips a persistent mesh edge, pulls it aside, takes genuine support steps, recoils and turns to reveal the parked black sedan. There is no instant image swap. However, the curtain reads too much like an authored sheet in the selected flat renderer; its keyed folds lack convincing material response. Stair/pelvis timing still feels mechanical. Lighting and silhouette separation are weak. The three-riser set is an isolated proof, not a coherent completed second-storey house layout.
