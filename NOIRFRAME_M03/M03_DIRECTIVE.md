# PHASE 3 — STAIRCASE: PHYSICAL CONTACT

The staircase is a priority failure.

## Environment

Create a consistent staircase with:

- Actual tread surfaces
- Risers
- Landing
- Railings
- Wall geometry
- Ceiling clearance
- Connected upper hallway
- Consistent perspective
- Correct character-to-stair scale

Every step must have a known world-space position and height.

## Foot contact algorithm

For each step:

1. Determine which foot supports the body.
2. Lock that foot to the current tread.
3. Transfer body weight toward the support foot.
4. Lift the free foot.
5. Move it along a controlled arc.
6. Place it on the next tread.
7. Establish contact before transferring weight.
8. Raise the pelvis to the new support height.
9. Release the previous foot.
10. Repeat with the opposite leg.

Do not use a flat-ground walking cycle on stairs.

Do not simply move the entire character diagonally upward.

## Additional failure cases

Check for:

- Feet passing through stair surfaces
- Feet floating above treads
- Feet sliding while planted
- Character climbing without actual vertical displacement
- Character skipping stairs accidentally
- Knees penetrating steps
- Legs stretching to reach steps
- Pelvis moving before support exists
- Character walking through the railing
- Camera hiding the feet
- Stair geometry changing between shots
- Inconsistent scale between lower and upper landings
- Repeated identical poses with no body-weight response

## Staircase diagnostics

Produce a side-view test with at least three consecutive steps.

Display:
- Foot contact markers
- Foot target positions
- Pelvis trajectory
- Supporting foot
- Current tread index
- IK joint positions

Use separate clean and diagnostic renders.

- Character entering through a closed door
- Character changing scale during camera cuts
- Left/right hand swapping between angles
